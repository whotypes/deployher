Let's be so real right now. We've had the cannon event. Open a dashboard one day, see a `$50/mo` bill for a pet project, and think "hold on. what the f**k am I even paying for?" a CDN? a build queue? s3 repackaged with a nice UI? And the most _crushing_ question of all. "im an engineer, couldn't i just... build this myself?"

That's where the conviction for my fun little sidequest ~~pdploy~~ (now **[Deployher](https://deployher.com)**) stems. It's live at **deployher.com** now: split-domain production (`deployher.com` marketing, `dash.deployher.com` app, `api.deployher.com` API, `*.deployher.com` previews), [open source on GitHub](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher), same stack you can run on your own box. I'm not competing with [Railway](https://railway.com/) or [Dokploy](https://dokploy.com/) or [Coolify](https://coolify.io/). Those exist. They're good. I'm not trying to beat them in a feature matrix. User 0 was always me. I launch small stateful projects constantly, and at some point "just give me a deploy button I control" became easier than negotiating another SaaS invoice.

### The enshittification of everything

Storytime.

In the fall of 2022, <LinkPreview url="https://www.heroku.com/blog/next-chapter">Heroku</LinkPreview> killed their free tier. Salesforce's official line was fraud and abuse. [TechCrunch covered it](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/25/heroku-announces-plans-to-eliminate-free-plans-blaming-fraud-and-abuse/). The [Dev Center changelog](https://devcenter.heroku.com/changelog-items/2502) is blunt: as of November 28, 2022, free dynos and hobby-dev Postgres/Redis were gone.

After a decade of "just `git push` and it works bro" Salesforce announced they were "sunsetting free product plans" starting November 28th, 2022. Dynos from $7/month, Postgres at $9.

The thing that taught a generation of developers how to deploy became a shell of itself. A year later, <LinkPreview url="https://blog.replit.com/new-limits-and-plans">Replit</LinkPreview> tightened limits too. Same playbook, slightly different excuses.

Their announcement pointed to "more powerful infrastructure" "crypto mining abuse" and users burning "thousands of dollars of bandwidth" The free plan survived, but barely. (Although they did outline some fair reasons.)

<Callout type="custom" title="An aside on free tiers" borderColorClass="border-blue-500" icon="warning" backgroundColorClass="bg-blue-100">

I don't want to be too dramatic here. These are businesses; obviously they need to make money. Servers cost money. Abuse costs money. GPUs cost stupid money.

But I also think the “users ruined the free tier” framing is letting the companies off the hook. Because subsidized growth is not something new. In fact, I might even say a tale as old as time. A company gives away something generous, users flood in, the graph goes up, investors smile, and everyone agrees to have amnesia and figure it out later.

Then later shows up.

Suddenly, the same users who helped guide a product to prominence are recast as the problem. The usage that once looked like "we're a16z bound" now looks like abuse. The story quietly shifts from “look at how much DevRel we do” to “people are burning thousands in bandwidth, daily.”

And yes, some people are actually abusing it. Mining crypto on free accounts is insane behavior. Spinning up ten accounts to dodge limits is obviously lame. I’m not defending that. But most users are not running some evil bandwidth cartel. They are just using the product under the limits the company itself created.

That is the part I find annoying.

The user did not invent the business model. The user did not set the limits. The user did not decide to subsidize usage for growth, community, market share, or investor screenshots. The company did.
</Callout>

Sound familiar? [Cory Doctorow gave it a name](https://pluralistic.net/2023/01/21/potemkin-ai/#hey-look-over-there): enshittification. Platforms start amazing for users. Permissive free tiers, almost too good to be true. They extract value from users to attract business; then from everyone to maximize profit. And I'm sad to say, this ain't new. From Heroku to Netflix going from ["love is sharing a password"](https://x.com/netflix/status/840276073040371712) to, well, you know.

However, it's not all doom. And if the SaaS stocks are any indication, investors are getting a little nervous.

### PaaS 101

Every PaaS (think [Vercel](https://vercel.com/), [Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/), the ghost of [Heroku](https://www.heroku.com/)) is really just a handful of core pieces you can run on your own metal. Dashboards and GitHub buttons are nice. They're not the hard part.

1. <span className="text-white">Web server</span> → Handle deploy requests.
2. <span className="text-white">Build queue</span> → Ensure builds happen in order.
3. <span className="text-white">Build worker</span> → Isolate builds in containers.
4. <span className="text-white">Object storage</span> → Store build artifacts (HTML, JS, etc)
5. <span className="text-white">Preview server</span> → Serve the built assets on a unique subdomain.
6. <span className="text-white">Authentication</span> → Secure access to deploys and persistent sessions.
7. <span className="text-white">Database</span> → Track users, projects, and deploys.

Everything else, like the web dashboards, GitHub integrations, log streaming, pretty graphs, is just nice to have on top. Deployher is framework agnostic too, so you can make your build queue use [RabbitMQ](https://www.rabbitmq.com/), [Kafka](https://kafka.apache.org/), or even a custom queue you build yourself. Same with object storage, like [S3](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/), [R2](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/), dare I say [MinIO](https://min.io/)?

### Choosing violence in a tech stack (Bun)

I went all-in on [Bun](https://bun.sh/) proudly. Not as a package manager. As the runtime. Server, workers, tests, the lot. <LinkPreview url="https://bun.sh/blog/bun-v1.3">Bun 1.3</LinkPreview> was the tipping point for me: built-in Redis client, better HTTP ergonomics, native S3. Then [Anthropic acquired Bun](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-acquires-bun-as-claude-code-reaches-usd1b-milestone) in late 2025. [Jarred's post](https://bun.com/blog/bun-joins-anthropic) is worth reading if you care about where JS runtimes are heading. I didn't pick Bun because Anthropic might win the agent wars. I picked it because `import { S3Client } from "bun"` beats dragging in half of npm for artifact uploads.

The S3 client is the thing I still brag about. Deployher stores build output in [Garage](https://garagehq.deuxfleurs.fr/), a self-hostable S3-compatible store from the [Deuxfleurs](https://deuxfleurs.fr/) collective. [They've been running it in production since 2020](https://garagehq.deuxfleurs.fr/blog/2022-introducing-garage/). AGPL-licensed. Designed for "a few nodes in a closet," not "pretend we're AWS." Good enough for artifact storage and avatar buckets. In Node you'd reach for [`@aws-sdk/client-s3`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@aws-sdk/client-s3). In Bun:

```typescript
import { S3Client } from "bun";

const client = new S3Client({
  endpoint: config.s3.endpoint,
  region: config.s3.region,
  bucket: config.s3.bucket,
  accessKeyId: config.s3.accessKeyId,
  secretAccessKey: config.s3.secretAccessKey,
  virtualHostedStyle: false
});

await client.file(`${artifactPrefix}/index.html`).write(data, { type: "text/html" });
```

That's the whole vibe. No command objects. No six subpackages. Redis is the same story: raw [`XADD`](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/xadd/) / [`XREADGROUP`](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/xreadgroup/) over a connection, plus [`EVAL`](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/eval/) for Lua when you need atomic slot accounting. I benchmarked it against [ioredis](https://github.com/redis/ioredis) once on our stream workload and Bun was faster. Doesn't matter at our scale. Still satisfying.

The dependency tree is not the six-package fairy tale from the prototype anymore. The dashboard grew into a real app: [Vite](https://vite.dev/), [shadcn/ui](https://ui.shadcn.com/), [TanStack Query](https://tanstack.com/query), [React Router](https://reactrouter.com/). The build pipeline still leans on Bun for I/O. See [package.json in the repo](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/package.json) if you want the full list. Bigger than a demo, still not `node_modules` as a lifestyle.

## Under the hood (without turning this into README cosplay)

I'm not going to paste half the repo here. If you want file-level detail, [docs/SETUP.md](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/docs/SETUP.md) and [docs/DEPLOYMENT.md](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/docs/DEPLOYMENT.md) exist for a reason. What follows is the shape of the thing, and why I made annoying choices.

### Boot and config

`src/index.ts` starts Bun.serve, optionally builds the Vite client in production (`SKIP_CLIENT_BUILD=1` in Docker), pings S3, schedules queue stall alerts, and nags the preview-runner to rewarm server previews after a restart. Dev mode skips the client build and assumes you're on `bun run dev:vite` for HMR.

Config is layered: [config/default.toml](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/config/default.toml), optional `config/local.toml`, then env vars for secrets. Split-domain production needs `DEPLOYHER_PRIMARY_DOMAIN`, per-hostname env vars, and `BETTER_AUTH_URL` on the API origin. Preview URLs slug project names; individual deploys get short base-36 IDs so `{shortId}.deployher.com` doesn't look like a UUID got loose.

### Router

Still no Express. Still hand-rolled in [`src/router.ts`](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/src/router.ts). ~580 lines now because GA added CSRF, CORS, CLI bearer auth, observability APIs, and a pile of `/api/ui/*` routes for the SPA.

The rule that saved my sanity: **check the Host header before path matching**. Preview traffic hits `{shortId}.yourdomain` first. [Caddy](https://caddyserver.com/) on the `edge` container handles host routing in production; Bun still serves previews and API. Dashboard routes like `/projects/:id` return the Vite shell from `dist/client/index.html`; React fetches JSON separately. The early dual HTML/JSON page renderer is dead. Good riddance.

### Database

[Drizzle](https://orm.drizzle.team/) on [Postgres](https://www.postgresql.org/). Schema in [`src/db/schema.ts`](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/src/db/schema.ts). Better Auth tables, plus projects, env vars, deployments, deployment events, preview traffic, alert rules. The `deployments` row is where the drama lives: build strategy, serve strategy, artifact prefix, runtime image digest, preview resolution metadata, heartbeats, retry counts. `currentDeploymentId` on projects is how "promote this deploy" works. Not magic. Just a pointer you can swing backward.

Migrations in `/drizzle/`, applied on **app-api** startup when `RUN_MIGRATIONS=1`.

### The queue

Deploy jobs go through [Redis Streams](https://redis.io/docs/latest/develop/data-types/streams/) consumer groups, not [BullMQ](https://docs.bullmq.io/). BullMQ is fine. I didn't need 15k lines of framework for six commands: `XADD`, `XREADGROUP`, `XACK`, `XAUTOCLAIM`, and friends. Implementation lives in `src/queue.ts`.

The part I actually had to think about: **per-account concurrency**. Without it, one person queues fifty builds and everyone else waits. Deployher uses a Lua script (via Redis `EVAL`) to grant slots in a set keyed by user ID, with TTL so crashed workers don't leak capacity forever. When a slot isn't available, the job gets deferred: re-enqueued with a fresh stream ID, old message ACK'd. If you just leave failed messages in the pending list you get infinite retry loops. Ask me how I know. (The deferral logic lives in [`src/queue.ts`](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/src/queue.ts) if you want to read the scars.)

Workers scale with `docker compose up -d --scale deployment-worker=4`. Crash recovery is [`XAUTOCLAIM`](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/xautoclaim/): steal idle jobs from dead consumers.

### The build worker

`src/workers/buildWorker.ts` is ~2,000 lines and the file I open when something catches fire at 1am. Yes, I know that's too long. No, I'm not splitting it before the next thing breaks.

Flow in plain English: dequeue job, mark building, heartbeat, download repo zipball from [GitHub's API](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/repos/contents#download-a-repository-archive-zip) (OAuth token from Better Auth), merge env vars, detect strategy, run install/build inside ephemeral [Docker](https://docs.docker.com/engine/) containers via [dockerode](https://github.com/apocas/dockerode), upload artifacts to Garage, push a runtime image to [Nexus](https://www.sonatype.com/products/sonatype-nexus-repository) (or your registry), update the row, stream logs over Redis pub/sub to [SSE](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Server-sent_events).

Strategies: Node (npm/pnpm/yarn/bun, including [Next.js](https://nextjs.org/) server mode when `.next/` exists), Python (pip/uv/mkdocs), static uploads, Dockerfile-first server builds. Build containers get labels like `io.deployher.build=true` so cleanup is a filter, not archaeology. Workdirs live under `/tmp/deployher-builds` on the host, bind-mounted into workers so the daemon sees the same paths.

**app-api never mounts the Docker socket.** Only **deployment-worker** and **preview-runner** do. That boundary mattered once server previews existed and I didn't want the control plane one rogue container away from root on the host.

### Storage and bootstrap

Garage runs in Compose (`dxflrs/garage:v2.2.0`). Artifacts bucket + avatar bucket. Multipart uploads for big build output. Incremental log reads via byte offsets so the log viewer doesn't re-fetch megabytes of stdout every poll.

The old bash bootstrap script is gone. The **`deployher`** CLI (`bun run deployher bootstrap` on a VPS, `deployher start` on a laptop with demo seed) brings up Postgres, Redis, Garage, Nexus, runs migrations in `oven/bun` without requiring Bun on the host, syncs base images, starts edge/app-api/marketing/workers/runner. Production env rollouts: `deployher ops storage-env-deploy`.

### Previews

Three ways to hit a deploy:

1. `{shortId}.deployher.com` (wildcard DNS)
2. `/d/{shortId}/…` path fallback
3. `/preview/{deploymentId}/…` for server runtimes via **preview-runner**

Static files serve from S3 through Bun with sensible cache headers (immutable hashed assets, `no-cache` HTML). Server apps run in bounded containers the runner pulls by registry digest. Next.js, Bun APIs, Dockerfile deploys. The S3 tarball path still exists for older rows but new deploys push to the registry.

### UI and auth

Three surfaces:

- [`apps/marketing`](https://astro.build/): Astro landing at the apex
- `dist/client`: Vite React dashboard
- `src/`: Bun API

The prototype was streaming SSR with almost no client JS. Cute. GA needed env editors, observability charts, promote buttons, drag-and-drop static uploads. I caved. Bun owns API and previews; React owns the app UI. Fair trade.

[Better Auth](https://www.better-auth.com/) handles GitHub OAuth, email/password, sessions, and [device authorization](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8628) for `deployher login`. Split origins in production. GitHub scope includes `repo` for private zipballs. Avatars go to a separate S3 bucket.

## What's running in production

deployher.com is the same Compose stack you get self-hosting, just with real DNS and TLS.

| Host | Role |
|------|------|
| `deployher.com` | Marketing |
| `dash.deployher.com` | Dashboard SPA |
| `api.deployher.com` | Auth + API |
| `*.deployher.com` | Previews |

Services: **edge** (Caddy), **app-api**, **marketing**, **preview-runner**, **deployment-worker** (scaled), **garage**, **nexus**, **postgres**, **redis**, plus init images for Node/Bun toolchains. I run it on [Hetzner](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud) because the invoice is boring. ~4GB RAM for the full stack if you're reproducing this at home. Wildcard DNS to the VPS, TLS in front of edge, GitHub OAuth callback on `api.yourdomain.com`. [docs/SPLIT_DOMAIN.md](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/docs/SPLIT_DOMAIN.md) has the checklist if you're copying the layout.

Self-host path: clone, `deployher bootstrap`, fill OAuth secrets, point `*.yourdomain.com` at the box. Same code path we use for production. No separate "enterprise edition" fantasy.

## Things I learned (the hard way)

### Docker socket access is a privilege, not a personality trait

Everyone warns you about "Docker-in-Docker." What we actually do is mount `/var/run/docker.sock` into the worker and talk to the host daemon through [dockerode](https://github.com/apocas/dockerode). Ephemeral build containers, bind-mounted workdirs, label-based cleanup. The gotcha that actually got me: the path on the host has to match what the daemon sees. `/tmp/deployher-builds` on the worker is `/tmp/deployher-builds` on the host, full stop. [Docker's own docs](https://docs.docker.com/engine/security/#docker-daemon-attack-surface) are very clear that this is basically root. That's why **app-api** doesn't get the socket. Only workers and preview-runner.

### Redis Streams beat a queue framework for this job

If your semantics are "FIFO, consumer groups, crash recovery," [Redis Streams](https://redis.io/docs/latest/develop/data-types/streams/) is enough. [BullMQ](https://docs.bullmq.io/) is great software. I didn't need a framework-sized dependency for [`XADD`](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/xadd/), [`XREADGROUP`](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/xreadgroup/), [`XACK`](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/xack/), and [`XAUTOCLAIM`](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/xautoclaim/). The interesting bit was never the stream. It was not letting one user hog the build farm.

### Bun's S3 client is still the best "why Bun" answer

No [`@aws-sdk/*`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@aws-sdk/client-s3) dependency tree. No credential provider chain cosplay. [`import { S3Client } from "bun"`](https://bun.sh/docs/api/s3) and upload artifacts. Multipart writer for big files. Byte-offset log reads. Boring infrastructure that stays boring.

### I was wrong about zero client JS

The early prototype used streaming SSR with almost no hydration. Romantic. Also wrong for GA. Env editors, observability charts, promote buttons, drag-and-drop static uploads: that's a real frontend. [Vite](https://vite.dev/) + React on the client, Bun for API and previews. Fine.

### Custom routers don't explode until they do, then they don't

Subdomain extraction + split-host edge routing + preview fallbacks: ~580 lines in [`src/router.ts`](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/src/router.ts) and I can still trace a request without seventeen middleware layers. Would I recommend this for a CRUD app? No. For a deploy platform where `Host` is load-bearing? Yeah, actually.

### Preview-runner separation saved my sleep

Build workers push images. [preview-runner](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/tree/main/src/preview-runner) pulls and runs them with TTL and memory caps. Crashy preview containers don't take down auth or the dashboard. Worth the extra Compose service.

## What's next

Deployher is GA, not done. Still on the list:

- **GitHub push auto-deploy:** no built-in [`push` webhook](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks) yet. Queue deploys from your own CI via the API or [`deployher login`](https://github.com/whotypes/deployher/blob/main/docs/SETUP.md#deployher-cli-reference) + remote deploy for now.
- **Build caching:** Docker layer cache and `node_modules` reuse between builds on the same worker. Right now every build is a fresh container with fresh pain.
- **Multi-node Garage:** single-node today. [Garage](https://garagehq.deuxfleurs.fr/documentation/cookbook/real-world/) supports replication when we need it.
- **Horizontal app scaling:** multiple `app-api` replicas behind shared Redis/Postgres. Preview-runner already isolates runtime load.

Shipped since the early draft of this post: server previews ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/), Bun APIs, Dockerfile deploys), [Caddy](https://caddyserver.com/) edge routing, split-domain production, promote/rollback via `currentDeploymentId`, observability webhooks, static drag-and-drop uploads, CLI device auth, avatar storage, and the [Astro](https://astro.build/) marketing site.

## The punchline

A deployment platform is still a build queue, object storage, and something that serves the result. Everything else is polish and invoice padding.

[Vercel](https://vercel.com/) wraps that in a global CDN and edge functions. [Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/) wraps it in forms and identity. [Railway](https://railway.com/) wraps it in a nice dashboard and a credit card. They're good products. The core you're renting is smaller than the line items suggest.

Deployher runs on a cheap VPS ([Hetzner](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud) for me; [deployher.com](https://deployher.com) runs the same stack). Node, Python, static uploads, Next.js server mode, Dockerfile-first deploys, GitHub OAuth, env vars, live build logs, subdomain previews, container-isolated builds. It's not Vercel. It's not trying to be. It's the thing I built because I got tired of paying rent on my own deploy button.

Building it taught me more about how deploy pipelines work than years of clicking "Deploy" ever did. Zipball download, build container, artifact upload, registry push, preview proxy, subdomain routing: once you've wired those yourself, SaaS pricing makes more sense and bothers you more.

PaaS isn't so bad. You just have to decide whether you're the customer or the builder. I'm both, apparently.
